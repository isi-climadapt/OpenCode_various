## 3. Concepts, ranked

1. **Who Left These Cubes?** RECOMMENDED. Hero: **Bramble the wombat**, grumpy, proud, owns The
   Best Rock in the bush. One morning there are cubes on his rock. Neat little brown cubes. Someone
   is messing with his rock. Calm friend: **Pip the quokka**, who smiles at everything and already
   knows. Suspects with funny alibis: Kangaroo (has a pouch, could carry cubes; it only holds her
   joey), a satin bowerbird (collects things; only blue things), a dung beetle (rolls balls, not
   cubes), Echidna (digs, doesn't build). Bramble stakes out the rock all night and catches the
   culprit: himself. Wombats leave cube poo on rocks and logs to say "this spot is mine". Closing
   gag: the dung beetle tries to roll a cube, and it won't roll. Facts spread: why wombat poo is
   cube-shaped (2018 study of the wombat gut), 80 to 100 cubes a night, wombats mark rocks and
   logs, quokkas, bowerbirds, dung beetles.
   Why: sits on the strongest all-year small-press lane for ages 3-5 (Who Pooped on Me? #386) with
   a real, famous fact no polished story owns; a title with 0 exact matches; and the Banjo engine
   unchanged. Risk: potty humor narrows the gift audience a little; the book keeps it gentle (no
   rude words, the reveal is a science fact).
2. **Who Took My Pouch?** A joey's pouch "is stolen" (a new baby sibling has moved in; real
   kangaroo biology). Huge gift lane, 0 exact title match. Weaker fit: sibling buyers skew 1-4, the
   lane splits by sister and brother, and the trade classics hold the top 10.
3. **A platypus mystery** (an egg in the riverbank). Real science, thin lane, but a spring launch
   beside Hemming's The Egg Thief. Hold.
4. Echidna or quokka hero: thin search demand ("echidna book for kids", "quokka books for kids" exist
   but story books there have 0 to 62 reviews and no hook). Better as cast.

## 5. Locked concept: Who Took My Pouch? (listing draft)

The user picked concept 2 on 24 Oct 2026. Extra checks run after the pick, same day:

**Hero and names.** Clover, a kangaroo joey (she: the "big sister" lane is the indie-friendly
side, *I AM A BIG SISTER*, 179 reviews, 2025, sits at #2,818). Calm friend: Hugo the quokka. Name
checks on Amazon.com Books: "Clover the kangaroo" (no such character; only a Chinese-language
"Clover Kangaroo" series and *Search for the Hidden Clover: Kangaroo Island*), "Hugo the quokka"
(none). Dropped: "Kip" (a *Kip the Kangaroo* indie series and *Kip The Joey*), "Pip the quokka"
(a coloring book and *Pip Doesn't Feel Like Smiling*), "Sunny" (quokka coloring titles).

**Title.** Who Took My Pouch? Exact phrase on Amazon.com Books: 0 matches (the nearest are *Who
Took My Sock*, 2 reviews, *Pouch!*, 352, *Ouch In My Pouch*, 62). Same "Who ... My ...?" shape as
the Banjo books, so the two series sit together as one Hattie Moss look.

**Age band (rule 16): Ages 3-5.**

- (a) Demand: sibling searches use single ages, not bands: "big sister books for 3 year old",
  "... for 4 year old", "... for 5 year old", "big brother books for 3 year old", "... 4 year
  old"; younger buyers type "for toddlers board book" and "for 2 year old" (board books, which KDP
  can't print). The three ages shoppers type for a story book are exactly 3, 4 and 5, and "story
  books for kids ages 3-5" is the banded story search.
- (b) Buyers: the sibling bestsellers show 1-3 (I Am a Big Sister), 2-3, 2-4 and 2-5 (You're the
  Biggest); kangaroo stories show baby-4 and 3-6 (Where's My Joey?). The lane skews younger than
  the Banjo books.
- (c) Fit: a 32-page story of about 550 words with a mystery and a facts spread is a 3-5 read, not
  a 1-3 board book, and customer data would pull a 1-3 label up anyway.

Locked: **Ages 3-5** everywhere (subtitle, keywords, KDP Reading Age 3 to 5, cover badge,
description, A+, alt text). No toddler or 2-year-old keyword.
