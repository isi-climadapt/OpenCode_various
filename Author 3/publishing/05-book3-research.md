## Verdict

**Lead concept: Who Shook My Tree?**, a night thunderstorm mystery about being brave in a storm,
Ages 3-5, upload mid to late November 2026. Storm books sell all year (they rank #10,000 to #30,000
in October, outside storm season), then peak with the US storm season and spring classroom weather
units from March to August, which is when Book 2's winter sales stop. It answers a real parent need
(fear of thunder), it works in either hemisphere, and Hemming has no storm book.

## 3. Competition in the storm lane

- Classics: Thunder Cake (1990), The Storm Book (1952), Franklin and the Thunderstorm. Gentle,
  realistic, grandparent or parent figure soothes the child.
- Nonfiction and readers: NatGeo Storms, Flash Crash Rumble and Roll (173, #171,507), Zap! Clap!
  Boom! (34, #229,069), Thunderstorms! My Incredible World (35, #389,098).
- Feelings books: Greta and the Dark Cloud (126, #999,000), The Brave Little Ghost and the
  Thunderstorm (4). Small press, low ranks.
- Gap: no funny, character-led storm story with suspects and a twist, and no Australian setting.
  Banjo's engine turns a scary night into a comedy, then gives kids a real coping tool (count from
  the flash to the boom).

## 4. Concepts, ranked

1. **Who Shook My Tree?** RECOMMENDED. A hot, sticky summer night. Banjo is about to do his
   biggest glide ever when BOOM, his tree shakes, a white FLASH lights the bush, and everything
   starts dripping. Somebody is shaking his tree. Suspects with funny alibis: Wombat (his tummy
   RUMBLES, but only when he's hungry), Cockatoo (flashing white wings, but she was only
   stretching), Possum (THUMPING on the bark, but he's hiding from the noise himself). Then the
   prime suspect: a **lyrebird** who says "BOOM!" perfectly. Caught! But lyrebirds copy every
   sound they hear, and she copied it from the sky. Olive, calm as ever, explains the
   thunderstorm and teaches Banjo to count from the flash to the boom: the numbers get bigger,
   the storm moves away. Everyone shelters together. Joy: after the rain, puddles full of moon,
   the bush smells fresh, frogs sing, Banjo's sparkliest glide. Closing gag: at sunrise a huge
   RUMBLE shakes the tree again. It's Olive, snoring. Lyrebird copies it.
   Why: an all-year lane with a spring and summer peak, a parent need, a title that's clear at a
   glance, and the Book 1 engine and cast with one new character.
   Facts spread: lightning and thunder, counting seconds (about 5 seconds for each mile), summer
   storms in Australia, lyrebirds copy almost any sound, frogs call after rain, the smell of rain
   (the word "petrichor" was coined by Australian scientists in 1964), koalas hug cool trees,
   sugar gliders shelter in hollows. All to be checked at the story stage.
2. **Who Fluffed My Tree?** Spring gum blossom: Banjo's tree turns fluffy overnight. Pretty and
   true, but it sits beside The Flower Thief and the search hook ("spring books") is crowded with
   trade titles. Possible later.
3. **Who Egged My Tree?** A spring egg appears in Banjo's hollow (a kookaburra's). Funniest title
   and the biggest spring searches, but rejected: The Egg Thief (5 Jan 2027) is an egg mystery in
   spring with a woodland hero.
4. **Who Decorated My Tree?** Christmas beetles and summer blossom "decorate" Banjo's tree. 0
   title matches, but rejected for 2026: Australian Christmas books don't sell on .com and the
   window is five weeks. Could be a Christmas 2027 book once the series has reviews.
5. Valentine's or Groundhog Day: short windows, weak fit with the natural-change engine.
