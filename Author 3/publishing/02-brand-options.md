### 1. Sugar glider (recommended)

The closest match to a squirrel: lives in a tree hollow, bushy tail, glides like a flying squirrel, huge round eyes that carry big emotions. US kids know them as pets, so no explaining needed. The Amazon.com story lane is close to empty. It's nocturnal, which gives a built-in story engine (day and night, the moon, stars) that works in either hemisphere and fixes the season-timing problem in the competitor study. Easy to keep consistent in AI art: grey fur, one black stripe from nose to tail, pink nose, cream belly.

### 2. Koala

Highest recognition with US buyers. A koala guarding and counting its gum leaves maps straight onto the squirrel-and-leaves engine. Downsides: the most crowded lane of the three, and a sleepy animal is a harder sell as a panicky hero (the contrast can be the joke, but it's harder to write).

### 3. Wombat

A grumpy, possessive, dramatic personality fits the hero role well. Cube-shaped poo is a real fact kids love and a ready-made back-matter page. Downsides: lives in a burrow, so less visual range than a tree; Jackie French's Diary of a Wombat owns the "wombat picture book" search.
