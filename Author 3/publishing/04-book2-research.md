## 4. Concepts, ranked

1. **Who Frosted My Tree?** ⭐ RECOMMENDED. Banjo wakes on a cold morning in the high country to
   find his gum tree, and the whole bush, turned white. Someone has "frosted" it. Suspects with
   funny alibis: the white cockatoo (white feathers everywhere), the possum, the wombat. Spike the
   echidna, a fourth suspect, has vanished, which makes things look worse. Olive, wrapped round her
   branch, already knows. Reveal: it's snow, and Spike is asleep in a burrow, hibernating. Closing
   gag: Banjo's first snowball. Facts spread: Australia's snow country and snow gums, which bush
   animals hibernate (echidna, mountain pygmy possum), how sugar gliders huddle to keep warm.
   Why: hits winter, snow and hibernation searches together, a Nov to Feb window every year, a
   lane with few funny story books, and the Book 1 engine and cast unchanged. Risk: The Snow
   Thief owns "snow" for Hemming fans; Banjo's book is snow arriving, not leaving, and its title,
   art and Australian setting differ.
2. **Whose Egg Is This?** (working title; "Who Laid This Egg?" is taken). A spring and Easter
   mystery: Banjo finds an egg, every bird denies it, the platypus did it. Strong hook, real
   science (mammals that lay eggs). Hold for Easter 2028: it would launch against Hemming's *The
   Egg Thief* (Jan 2027) with no reviews.
3. **A Valentine's mystery** (Banjo gets secret gumnut gifts; the admirer is Olive). Indie-friendly
   search page, but a 4-week window and no natural change. Possible short Book 4.
4. **Possum or Opossum?** An American opossum turns up and insists he's a possum. US appeal and
   an opossum fan base, but a small evergreen niche, no season and no natural-change reveal.
5. **An Australian summer Christmas.** Rejected for 2026: small demand ("christmas in australia
   book" titles at 0 to 11 reviews), a crowded category and too little time to gather reviews.

**Series decision.** Continue Banjo and Olive. Sequels sell off book 1 (Hemming: 4,217 reviews on
the original against 163 to 1,143 on sequels) and Australian picture books hold steady ranks on
.com. American appeal comes from the US season and school unit (winter, snow, hibernation), not
from US animals, whose niche (opossum) is small.
