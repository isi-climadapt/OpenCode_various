## Why this story

Same engine as the Banjo books: a natural thing the hero misreads as a crime, a calm friend who
knows, a line-up of suspects with funny alibis, nature as the reveal, a closing gag, a facts page.

The natural thing is **a new baby joey**. Clover's mom closes her pouch and cleans it before the
birth, the way real kangaroo moms do, and Clover decides somebody has taken it. Every suspect has
a pouch of their own, and each alibi is a true pouch fact (the wombat's faces backward, the
pelican's is a beak, the possum's baby has outgrown hers, the echidna grows hers for an egg). The
possum and echidna alibis lead Clover toward the answer: pouches are for babies. The emotional
theme is becoming a big sister: the fear of being replaced, then pride. The closing gag turns the
title back on the baby.

## Facts spread (pages 30-31)

Checked 24 Oct 2026 against Taronga Zoo, Wildlife Victoria fact sheets, NSW Field of Mars EEC, ScienceFocus and Ranger Rick (kangaroo pouch timing varies by species):

- **Joeys.** A newborn kangaroo is about the size of a jellybean. It crawls into its mom's pouch
  and stays there for months.
- **Out of the pouch.** A joey leaves the pouch for good at about 8 to 10 months old, but it
  still pokes its head in to drink milk.
- **Two kinds of milk.** A kangaroo mom can make two kinds of milk at once: one for her tiny
  baby and one for her big joey.
- **Spring cleaning.** Before a new baby is born, a kangaroo mom licks her pouch clean.
- **Wombats** have a backward-facing pouch, so no dirt gets in when they dig.
- **Pelicans** scoop up fish and water in the pouch under their beak, then tip the water out.
- **Brushtail possum babies** live in the pouch for about 4 to 5 months, then ride on mom's back.
- **Echidnas** lay one soft egg and keep it in a pouch that forms on their tummy.
- **Quokkas** are small wallabies. They have pouches too, and they always look like they're
  smiling.
