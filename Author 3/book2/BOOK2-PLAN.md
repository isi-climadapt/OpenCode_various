## Why this story

Same engine as Book 1: a natural change the hero misreads as a crime, a calm friend who knows the
answer, a line-up of suspects with funny alibis, nature as the reveal, a closing gag, a facts page.

The natural change is **the first snow**, plus a second mystery: a friend who vanishes because
she's **hibernating**. Snow is a rare event in the hills where Banjo lives, so he's never seen it.
That keeps the story honest: koalas and sugar gliders live in places that get the odd snowfall,
not in the alpine snowfields. It hits the US searches the research found (winter books for kids
3-5, snow books, hibernation books for preschool and kindergarten) and the December to February
classroom units.

### Pages 30-31: Facts spread

Heading: **True Facts from the Bush**

- **Australia gets snow.** Snow falls every winter in the Australian Alps, and now and then on
  hills and high plains further down.
- **Snow gums** are gum trees that grow high in the snowy mountains. Their bark peels into stripes
  of white, gray, green, orange and red.
- **Snowflakes** form when water freezes high in cold clouds. Most snowflakes have six sides.
- **Echidnas hibernate.** In the coldest places, an echidna can sleep through winter for months.
  Its body cools right down and its heart beats very slowly.
- **Mountain pygmy possums** hibernate under the snow in the Australian Alps for up to seven
  months.
- **Sugar gliders** curl up together in their tree hollows to keep warm. On very cold nights they
  can drop into a deep sleep called torpor, and save energy.
- **Koalas** have thick, woolly fur that keeps out cold and rain.
- **Wombats** stay warm in deep burrows under the ground.
- **Cockatoos** fluff up their feathers to trap warm air.

Art: Small spot illustrations of each animal, a snow gum branch and a large snowflake, on a cream
background.

Facts to verify against reliable sources before typesetting: echidna hibernation length (NSW
National Parks gives up to 28 weeks in alpine areas), mountain pygmy possum hibernation length,
sugar glider torpor (a wild field study found torpor on cold or rainy nights), snow gum bark
colors, koala fur insulation, six-sided snowflakes. Koalas don't live in the alpine snowfields
(one was seen in Kosciuszko National Park in 2016, the first in over 70 years), so the story never
names the Alps as Banjo's home.
