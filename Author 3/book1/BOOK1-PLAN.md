## Why this story

The engine from the copy plan: a natural change the hero misreads as a crime, a calm friend who knows the answer, a line-up of suspects with funny alibis, nature as the reveal, a closing gag, a facts page.

The natural change is **gum trees shedding their bark**. Eucalypts drop long ribbons of old bark and show smooth, coloured new bark underneath. It's a uniquely Australian sight, it looks great in pictures, it teaches something real, and it doesn't depend on US seasons. Sugar gliders live in gum-tree hollows and eat gum sap, so the hero's home is the thing that changes.

Rejected for book 1: a shrinking moon (moon phases). *Sugar Glider Lights a Way* by Maya Tatsukawa (Macmillan, Aug 2026, ISBN 1250903947) already has a sugar glider whose moon goes missing. Too close.

## Art style for Author 3 (different from Nicola Slater)

- Soft gouache and colored-pencil look with visible paper grain. Rounded, chunky character shapes. Gentle line work, not heavy outlines.
- Palette: eucalyptus greens, warm ochre and terracotta bark, cream new bark with pink and green patches, twilight violet and dusky blue skies, moonlight silver. Avoid her signature orange-red autumn palette.
- Most of the story happens at dusk and at night (Banjo is nocturnal), so the book's look is twilight and moonlight. That's a clear visual identity on the shelf.
- Page rhythm from the copy plan: full scenes alternate with white-ground vignettes. Every image leaves a clear, calm area for text.
- No text, letters or signs in any generated image. All words are typeset in code (pipeline rule 2).

### Pages 30-31: Facts spread

Heading: **True Facts from the Bush**

- **Why do gum trees peel?** Many gum trees (eucalyptus) shed their old outer bark as the trunk grows wider. The new bark underneath can be cream, pink, green, orange or gray. Peeling also gets rid of moss, lichen and bugs.
- **Scribbly gums** have squiggly lines on their bark. The squiggles are tunnels made by tiny moth caterpillars.
- **Sugar gliders** sleep all day and come out at night. A sugar glider weighs about as much as an apple. A stretchy flap of skin between its front and back legs lets it glide up to 150 feet (45 meters). Sugar gliders live in tree hollows in family groups and eat tree sap, nectar and insects.
- **Koalas** sleep up to 20 hours a day and eat mostly eucalyptus leaves.
- **Cockatoos** chew wood to keep their beaks in shape.
- **Echidnas** catch ants and termites with a long, sticky tongue.
- **Brushtail possums** sleep in tree hollows, and sometimes in people's roofs.
- **Wombats** make poop shaped like cubes.
- **Furry animals shed too.** Like most furry animals, sugar gliders lose old fur as new fur grows.

Art: Small spot illustrations of each animal and a strip of peeling bark, on a cream background.

Facts to re-verify against a reliable source before typesetting: glide distance, sugar glider weight, koala sleep hours, cockatoo beak wear, scribbly gum moth.

## Production approach for step 4

- **The CLI sends only the prompt** (openart 0.1.1): no aspect ratio, no resolution flag. Ideogram 4.5 image2image comes back at 2048 px by default (44 credits). A 2048 px image across an 8.75 in page with bleed prints at about 234 DPI. That's below the 300 DPI target, but painterly art holds up far better than line art at that density. Step 4 checks it in the KDP previewer before committing to all pages.
- **Character consistency test first:** a Banjo and Olive reference sheet, then three test pages on 2 or 3 models (Ideogram 4.5, Nano Banana Pro with up to 14 reference images, Seedream 4.5). Show the user the results and pick one. Budget about 500 credits.
- **Full run:** about 30 images (26 story page images, title and end vignettes, cover front art) at about 2 attempts each and about 44 credits each, so roughly 2,700 credits. Account balance on 17 Oct: 5,556 credits.
- **Typesetting:** text placed in each page's clear area in code. A friendly rounded body font plus a bold display font for the shouts, different from Author 1 (Baloo, Sniglet, Pacifico) and Author 2 (Lilita One, Fredoka). Candidates are open-licence Google Fonts and would need the user's OK to download.
- **Outputs:** interior PDF (8.625 x 8.75 in pages with bleed, 32 pages), cover wrap PDF (spine for 32 pages premium color), Kindle fixed-layout files, KDP copy-paste guide, A+ set with the alt text table.
- **Latest layout first (pipeline rule 15):** before each stage, check `git log -5 --stat`, `git status` and `git diff` for newer work from other sessions, start from the most recently changed script for that stage, and re-check before every checkpoint. Cover: carry over the thumbnail-first principles from commits 1b7d534 and ff0d00a (solid dark one-line title, hero cropped and enlarged so it reads at search-result size). The "COLORING BOOK / FOR KIDS AGES 3-8" band doesn't fit a story book; use a picture-book equivalent such as a small "Ages 3-5" badge.
- **KDP copy-paste guide keywords (pipeline rule 14):** 7 plain lines, no "Box 1:" labels, under the heading "Keywords (copy each line into its own keyword box, in order):".
