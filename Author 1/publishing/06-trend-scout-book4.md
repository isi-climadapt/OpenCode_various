## Ranked shortlist

1. **WINTER ANIMALS with a holiday crossover ("Snowy Cuties")** ⭐ RECOMMENDED. Cute snow and arctic animals (penguins, polar bears, arctic foxes, reindeer, snowy owls, seals) doing cozy winter things: sledding, hot cocoa, building snowmen, ice skating, with about 10 of 50 pages holiday-flavored (decorating a tree, presents, stockings) so it sells as a Christmas gift without being a Christmas-only book. Why: the selling window runs Nov to Feb instead of ending Dec 24; page 1 has no bold-easy winter book with facts; facts are rich and true (penguin huddles, polar bear black skin); fits the Cuties line (Aussie Cuties, Spooky-style rhyme). Risk: winter demand is lower than Christmas demand, and .com.au is in summer, so Australian sales will be light.
2. **CHRISTMAS CUTIES (pure Christmas)**. Highest seasonal demand in the US and a direct match for the Cozy Christmas formula. Risks: page 1 is crowded with 200 to 2K-review incumbents, the window ends Dec 24, and a book that goes live around Oct 30 has about four weeks to Black Friday with zero reviews.
3. **WOODLAND CUTIES (North American forest animals)**. Evergreen, US-native animals (raccoon, fox, bear, moose, beaver, skunk), facts angle open. Risk: three zero-review launches this week alone, and no seasonal lift before spring.
4. Sweet treats / food. Strong numbers but drifts from the animal brand. Later.
5. Farm. Toddler-skewed, lower ceiling.
